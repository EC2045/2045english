§ 0

Overview

Modern English is the dominant spoken and written language of Earth in the Eastern Calendar era. It evolved from 20th–21st century English through deliberate simplification, removing irregular forms that served no communicative purpose.

§ 1

Word Order

Modern English uses Object-Subject-Verb as its default sentence structure. This is the most common word order among the world's languages, and represents a natural evolution from older patterns.

Default Order — OSV

RULE Object → Subject → Verb
EXAMPLE Doctor I does.
→ "I want to become a Doctor."
EXAMPLE Cat he have.
→ "He has a cat."
EXAMPLE Claude you is.
→ "You are Claude."

Inverted Order — SVO EMPHASIS

When a speaker wishes to emphasize the subject's agency, or in poetic and formal registers, Subject-Verb-Object order is used instead.

RULE Inverted (emphatic): Subject → Verb → Object
OSV — NEUTRAL Doctor I does.
SVO — EMPHATIC I does Doctor.
§ 2

Regular Verbs

All general verbs follow a single regular conjugation pattern. No exceptions. No third-person singular -s. No irregular past forms outside the auxiliary class.

Conjugation Pattern

FormSuffixExample (come)Example (walk)
Present—comewalk
Past+edcomedwalked
Past Participle+tencometenwalkten
ABOLISHED Third-person singular -s is removed entirely. "She go", "He run", "It work" — all the same as any other person.
OLD ENGLISH — ABOLISHED She comes. He runs.
MODERN ENGLISH — CORRECT She come. He run.

Perfect Aspect

Perfect constructions use +ten participle with have, placed at end of clause (following OSV order).

PERFECT Comen I have. → "I have come."
PERFECT That Book readten she have. → "She has read that Book."

Note on have as a regular verb

As a general verb meaning "to possess", have is fully regular:

FormWord
Presenthave
Pasthaved
Past Participlehaveten
POSSESSION — PRESENT Cat he have. → "He has a cat."
POSSESSION — PAST Cat he haved. → "He had a cat."
§ 3

Be-Verbs

The be-verb is retained in limited form. Only four conjugations exist. The full paradigm of am/are/is/was/were/been is reduced to four essential forms.

THE FOUR PERMITTED FORMS be · is · was · been
FormWordUsage
Infinitive / ImperativebeCommand form only; also base form
Present (all persons)isI is, you is, they is — no distinction
Past (all persons)wasShe was, we was — no distinction
Past ParticiplebeenUsed in perfect constructions
ABOLISHED am, are, were — removed. is and was serve all persons.
OLD — ABOLISHED I am. You are. They were.
MODERN — CORRECT I is. You is. They was.

Imperative (be)

COMMAND Claude be. → "Be Claude." / "Become Claude."
COMMAND Happy be. → "Be happy."

Statements

PRESENT Claude you is. → "You are Claude."
PAST Happy they was. → "They were happy."
§ 4

Auxiliary Verbs

Auxiliary verbs are exempt from the regularity requirement. Their irregular forms are retained because they carry distinct modal meanings that cannot be derived from a base form alone.

RULE Auxiliaries may be irregular. Their traditional forms are preserved.
AuxiliaryForms RetainedNotes
willwill, wouldFuture / conditional
cancan, couldAbility / possibility
shallshall, shouldObligation / suggestion
maymay, mightPermission / uncertainty
have (aux)have, hadPerfect aspect only — distinct from verb have
be (aux)be, is, was, beenSee §3

have (auxiliary) vs have (verb)

These are two separate words that happen to share a spelling in present tense. Context and conjugation distinguish them:

AUXILIARY had — perfect There gone I had.
→ "I had gone there."
VERB haved — possession Car she haved.
→ "She had a car."
§ 5

Nouns & Pronouns

Capitalization

RULE All nouns begin with a capital letter. Pronouns do not.
EXAMPLE Water he drink. — Water is a Noun (capital). he is a pronoun (lowercase).
EXAMPLE Dream they have.

Plural Forms

All plurals are regular. Historical irregular plurals are abolished.

RULE Add -s normally. Add -es when the word ends in a sound that would make pronunciation difficult.

§ 6

Questions

Interrogative sentences are marked with enclosing punctuation. No word order change is required to form a question.

RULE Enclose the sentence in ¿ … ? to mark it as a question. Word order does not change.
STATEMENT Doctor you does.
→ "You want to be a Doctor."
QUESTION ¿Doctor you does?
→ "Do you want to be a Doctor?"
QUESTION ¿Happy you is? → "Are you happy?"
QUESTION ¿Water she drink? → "Does she drink water?"
§ 7

Special Words

does — desire / want NEW MEANING

The word does has been repurposed. It is no longer a conjugation of do. Instead, it carries the meaning of want or wish to become, absorbing those words entirely.

RULE does = "want" / "wish to be/become". The words want and wish (in this sense) are no longer used.
EXAMPLE Doctor I does. → "I want to be a Doctor."
EXAMPLE Peace they does. → "They want peace."
QUESTION ¿Freedom you does? → "Do you want freedom?"

did — past of do RETAINED

The word did is retained as the past tense of the verb do (meaning "to perform an action"). This is kept for phonetic reasons — doed is considered difficult to pronounce.

EXAMPLE That she did. → "She did that."
